External primary sources:
Burtch, G., Moakler, R., Gordon, B. R., Zhang, P., and Hill, S. (2025).
Characterizing and Minimizing Divergent Delivery in Meta Advertising Experiments.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21251
https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21251v1
Also checked the November 12, 2025 MSI report, especially pp. 23-26:
https://thearf-org-unified-admin.s3.amazonaws.com/MSI/2025/MSI_Report_25-140.pdf
Our matching settings and small age SMDs do not establish elimination of divergent delivery.
The report's strongest configurations use awareness/reach and constrained frequency;
our manuscript describes link-click optimization. Its age/gender t-stat diagnostic is
calibrated on awareness-optimized native A/B tests, not our matched separate campaigns.

Lodish et al. (1995). How T.V. Advertising Works: A Meta-Analysis of 389 Real World
Split Cable T.V. Advertising Experiments. Journal of Marketing Research 32(2), 125-139.
https://doi.org/10.1177/002224379503200201
Publisher abstract supports the budget-increase statement. No unverified share of
insignificant experiments is quoted. Historical study, not proof advertising never works.

Simonov, A., Nosko, C., and Rao, J. M. (2018). Competition and Crowd-Out for Brand
Keywords in Sponsored Search. Marketing Science 37(2), 200-215.
https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2017.1065
Author-hosted article: https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/26238/competition_crowdout.pdf
Includes both low incrementality without competition and value of defensive advertising.

Blake, T., Nosko, C., and Tadelis, S. (2015). Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search
Effectiveness: A Large-Scale Field Experiment. Econometrica 83(1), 155-174.
https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA12423
Author-hosted version: https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/stadelis/BNT_ECMA_rev.pdf
Brand ads: no measurable short-term benefit. Nonbrand: new/infrequent users respond;
average short-term returns negative. Explicitly label the economics journal and setting.

Shapiro, B. T., Hitsch, G. J., and Tuchman, A. E. (2021).
TV Advertising Effectiveness and Profitability: Generalizable Results from 288 Brands.
Econometrica 89(4), 1855-1879. https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA17674
Previously circulated as Generalizable and Robust TV Advertising Effects (2020):
https://www.nber.org/papers/w27684
Author manuscript: https://economics.sas.upenn.edu/system/files/2021-02/Ad-Effects-Generalizable-Robust-Revision-3-Jan-29.pdf
Publisher abstract: more than 80% of brands have negative estimated marginal ROI.
Manuscript conclusion (printed p. 23): two thirds of estimated effects are not
statistically distinguishable from zero. This is not evidence that all those effects
are exactly zero. Observational identification with fixed effects and a TV-market
border robustness strategy; not a randomized experiment. Marginal ROI concerns extra
advertising at observed spending levels, not whether all advertising should stop.
